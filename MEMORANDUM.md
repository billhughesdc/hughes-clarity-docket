# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** File — Hughes Clarity Docket  
**FROM:** Bill Hughes, Esq.  
**DATE:** 16 September 2026  
**RE:** Legislative audit of the Senate’s 15 September 2026 failure to proceed on the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (market-structure bill)

---

## I. Purpose and method

This memorandum is an **audit of the legislative event**, not a lobbyist’s scorecard and not a product pitch. It relies on the public roll-call posture as reported on 15 September 2026, contemporaneous floor statements, and the known architecture of federal digital-asset oversight. Where a fact is taken from press accounts, it is labeled as such. Where it is legal analysis, it is labeled as such.

I have not been retained by the Senate, the House, the SEC, or the CFTC to write this. I write it because the vote left a **record gap**: markets still exist; the statute does not.

---

## II. What was before the Senate

The vehicle commonly styled the **Digital Asset Market Clarity Act** (the “Clarity Act”) was market-structure legislation: an attempt to assign primary federal jurisdiction over categories of digital assets, to set registration and disclosure baselines, and to reduce the present condition in which the same instrument can be treated as a commodity, a security, or neither, depending on the forum.

Public statements by the Majority Leader framed the bill as a successor step after last year’s stablecoin legislation (commonly referred to as the GENIUS Act). That framing is politically useful and legally incomplete. Stablecoin issuance rules do not answer **spot-market structure** for bitcoin, ether, solana, or the long tail of EVM tokens.

The Senate was not voting the bill up or down on final passage. It was voting a **motion to proceed**. In the modern Senate, that motion is the sixty-vote gate.

---

## III. The vote

**Date of vote:** 15 September 2026 (Tuesday).  
**Question:** Motion to proceed to consideration of the Clarity Act.  
**Result, as publicly reported:** **49 yeas, 50 nays.**  
**Threshold:** 60.  
**Disposition:** Failed.

Reported Republican nos included Sens. Collins (Me.), Hawley (Mo.), Moran (Kan.), and Tillis (N.C.). The Democratic caucus was reported as unified in opposition. I take these names from contemporaneous press; the official Senate roll call is the controlling exhibit when posted by the Clerk.

**Audit finding 1.** The bill did not “lose on the merits of bitcoin.” It lost a **procedural supermajority**. Treating 49–50 as a national referendum on digital assets is sloppy advocacy and sloppy law.

**Audit finding 2.** Failure to obtain even a simple majority, if the 49–50 tally is confirmed on the Clerk’s site, is politically worse than a 59–41 cloture loss. It means the majority party did not hold its own conference. That is a **whip and text** problem.

---

## IV. The stated reason for lost Democratic votes

Floor and press accounts on 15 September 2026 locate the break in **ethics provisions** — specifically, whether the text adequately addressed conflicts arising from official and family financial interests in digital-asset ventures, including those associated with the President.

Senator Warren’s public opposition, and Senator Warnock’s comments regarding “loopholes,” are consistent with that account. Senator Lummis, a principal author, was reported as saying the effort was “over” after more than a year and after a large number of Democratic text requests had been accepted.

**Audit finding 3.** Ethics and market structure are both legitimate legislative objects. Binding them so that **neither becomes law** is a failure of bicameral craft. A conflicts statute can be written as Title II. A market-structure statute can be written as Title I. Conferees chose, or were forced into, a single pile of paper.

**Audit finding 4.** The undersigned does **not** ask Congress to ignore official self-dealing. This docket asks Congress to **write the ethics rules so they pass**, and to **write the market rules so they pass**. Retail holders of bitcoin, ether, and solana are not parties to anyone’s family balance sheet. They should not be collateral.

---

## V. What the failure leaves in force

Until Congress enacts a statute, the following remain true as a matter of practice:

1. **SEC** continues to police offerings and intermediaries under the securities laws, including by enforcement and by staff statements that do not bind courts.
2. **CFTC** continues to treat certain digital commodities as within its anti-fraud and, in some settings, jurisdictional reach, without a clean spot-market charter of the kind the bill sought.
3. **State money-transmission and banking** overlays continue to differ by venue.
4. **Retail users** continue to self-custody bitcoin (UTXO), EVM assets (account model), and solana assets (high-throughput L1) under a federal silence that helps neither consumer protection nor innovation.

**Audit finding 5.** “We will do it at the agencies” is not a substitute for a statute when the agencies disagree about the same token. Joint rulemaking without a congressional allocation is an invitation to years of APA litigation.

---

## VI. What a passable bill must still do

Counsel’s punch list, stripped of caucus talking points:

| Element | Why it belongs in statute |
|---------|---------------------------|
| Definition of digital commodity vs. security | Courts need a test that is not “we know it when we sue.” |
| Registration of spot platforms | Retail needs a licensed venue option without banning self-custody. |
| Custody and segregation | Exchange insolvency law is still too much case-by-case. |
| Issuer / foundation disclosure | Especially for EVM token systems with admin keys. |
| Staking and protocol rewards | Solana and proof-of-stake ether cannot live in a footnote. |
| Bitcoin’s commodity character | Should be stated, not left to vibe. |
| Ethics / recusal / reporting | Covered officials and immediate family — enforceable, not cosmetic. |

A bill that solves only the first column and waves at the last will fail again. A bill that solves only the last and leaves bitcoin, ether, and solana in a jurisdictional fog will also fail the public.

---

## VII. Docket posture

This file therefore supports the Hughes Clarity Docket:

- Revive negotiations in this Congress if the calendar still permits.  
- If it does not, **pre-file** a clean two-title vehicle for the next Congress.  
- Do not tell retail holders that the law is “done” because one motion to proceed failed on a Tuesday in September.
