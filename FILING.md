# FILING

**To:** The Majority Leader of the United States Senate; the Minority Leader of the United States Senate; the Speaker of the House of Representatives; the Minority Leader of the House of Representatives; the Chair and Ranking Member of the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs; the Chair and Ranking Member of the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry; the Chair and Ranking Member of the House Financial Services Committee.

**From:** Bill Hughes, Esq., Washington, D.C., as counsel of record on this public docket, on behalf of persons who join the file.

**Re:** Revival and enactment of digital-asset market-structure legislation following the Senate’s 15 September 2026 failure to proceed on the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act.

**Date:** 16 September 2026

---

## Prayer for relief

The undersigned respectfully requests that the Congress:

1. **Treat the 15 September 2026 motion-to-proceed as interlocutory, not terminal.** A 49–50 vote is a failure to obtain sixty votes. It is not a merits judgment on whether the United States needs a market-structure statute.

2. **Reopen bicameral negotiations immediately** on a vehicle that can command sixty votes in the Senate in this Congress or, failing that, be teed up for the next.

3. **Separate, in drafting, (a) market-structure rules** (spot-market oversight, registration, custody, issuer disclosures, secondary-market trading) **from (b) ethics and conflict rules** applicable to covered officials and their families. Both belong in law. Collapsing them into a single kill-switch served neither retail holders nor the public integrity interest.

4. **Preserve a dual-track but coherent allocation** between the Commodity Futures Trading Commission and the Securities and Exchange Commission, with a statutory definition of digital commodities versus securities that a district court can apply without a new enforcement theory every quarter.

5. **Protect retail access** — including self-custody, on-chain settlement, and lawful secondary trading — rather than writing a statute that only large intermediaries can survive.

6. **Direct the agencies**, pending enactment, to publish a joint interpretive timetable so that the failure of cloture is not treated as a green light for regulation-by-enforcement as a substitute for legislation.

## Grounds (summary)

The grounds are set out at length in the accompanying **Memorandum of Legislative Audit**. In short:

- American households already hold bitcoin, ether and other EVM assets, and solana-ecosystem tokens. They are entitled to a **readable statute**, not a permanent circuit split by press release.
- The House has previously advanced market-structure text. The Senate’s procedural failure on 15 September 2026 returned the file to uncertainty.
- Public reporting identifies ethics language — not the existence of bitcoin, ether, or solana markets themselves — as the immediate cause of lost votes. That is a **drafting and negotiation failure**, not a reason to abandon the field.
- In the absence of statute, retail users remain exposed to venue risk, custody ambiguity, and selective enforcement. Counsel cannot give clean advice. That is a rule-of-law problem.

---

## What this filing is not

This filing does **not** ask any person to buy or sell any digital asset. It does **not** endorse any token, protocol, or campaign. It does **not** waive ethics concerns; it asks that they be written as **enforceable ethics law**, not used as an excuse to leave markets without a statute.
